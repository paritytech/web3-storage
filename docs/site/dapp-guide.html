Scalable Web3 Storage / dApp guide

dApp developer guide

How an application stores, reads and verifies data. Your client uploads data to providers over HTTP and submits transactions to the storage pallet only for setup, checkpoints and disputes. Names on this page follow design v2.6. Check the runtime metadata of the chain you connect to for the exact call signatures.

What you can rely on

  • After commit: the provider returns signatures over the new bucket state (MMR root) that includes your data, bound to your agreement (agreement_id) and to this chain. With these signatures you can challenge the provider on-chain (challenge_offchain) while the agreement is live. You get this guarantee without a transaction.
  • After checkpoint: the MMR root is on-chain. Anyone can challenge the providers that signed it (on a private bucket: members and primary-agreement owners), and the commitment remains in chain history if you lose the signatures.
  • Stake: each agreement records the provider's stake at creation, and the provider stays liable at that amount until the agreement ends, whatever it sets for new agreements later. A physical provider that does not answer a challenge by the deadline loses its entire stake; for a virtual provider, each liable member loses per_provider_stake. An invalid answer is rejected and the challenge stays open.
  • Stake under challenge: you can challenge only while the agreement is live. The stake your agreement recorded stays locked until the agreement expires, and longer while any challenge against the provider is open: the provider cannot lower it or deregister (deregister_provider) until the challenge is resolved. A challenged virtual provider keeps the stake of all its members locked.
  • Duration: agreements bind both sides until expiry. The provider cannot leave early. You cannot cancel and reclaim the payment. A provider may refund the remaining payment (refund_agreement); the agreement then continues unpaid and the provider stays liable.
  • After a slash: a slash bumps the provider's version and last_stake_event, so you see it from provider state. If a slash leaves a physical provider with less stake than your live agreement recorded, anyone can call remove_slashed and the remaining payment returns to you.

The chain does not check that providers serve reads fast. Your client checks that, and punishes a slow provider with a burn at settlement, a challenge, or by not extending the agreement.

Concepts

TermMeaning
BucketOn-chain container with a permanent bucket_id. It lists members, providers and the current snapshot. Providers can change; the id does not.
RolesAdmin (members, settings, delete data), Writer (append), Reader (read, only meaningful on a private bucket). Each role includes the next.
VisibilityPublic or Private. On a private bucket, primaries serve reads only to members. See Private buckets.
Primary providerAdded by the admin. Receives writes from clients and signs checkpoints. A bucket has at most MaxPrimarySlots (8) signing slots: a physical primary takes one, a virtual primary one per member.
Replica providerAdded by any account that gets a replica quote from the provider (the quote names that account). Syncs data from other providers, confirms sync on-chain, and receives a payment per confirmation. No limit per bucket.
Virtual providerOne provider identity backed by several physical providers (members) that share its stake. It has a synthetic account and you address it like a physical provider. Its quotes, commitments and checkpoint signatures are sets of at least k member signatures. It has no address of its own: you connect to a member. ProviderInfoResponse reports per_provider_stake, k and member_count.
Storage agreementOne per provider per bucket, with a per-bucket agreement_id: quota (max_bytes), prepaid payment, the provider's stake at creation, expiry, role, and owner. The payment is held on the owner until settlement.
data_rootMerkle root of a chunk tree. Chunks are at most 256 KiB and addressed by blake2_256 hash.
MMR and commitmentEach bucket has one Merkle Mountain Range. Each leaf holds a data_root. A Commitment is (mmr_root, start_seq, leaf_count).
CheckpointA commitment written on-chain with signatures from at least min_providers signing slots (physical signers; a virtual primary's members count individually).
ChallengeAn on-chain request that forces a provider to post one chunk with Merkle proofs, or lose its stake.

bucket://42/<data_root>              immutable: pins exact content
bucket://42/latest/leaf/17            mutable: current leaf 17

Your client looks up the bucket on-chain (bucket_info, bucket_agreements) to find which providers store it and their agreements.

Integration flow

Diagram of the whole flow: 04 dApp integration flow

  1. Choose providers

    Discovery is off-chain. Page the runtime API providers(offset, limit) (or read one with provider_info) and filter and rank the ProviderInfoResponse entries yourself: settings (price, duration limits, accepting flags), stake (the figure for new agreements), locked_stake, committed_bytes, stats, and version. Record version: you pass it as expected_version in every call that binds you to the provider's terms.

    Compare stake, agreements extended against agreements burned and refunded, and challenges_failed. Do not mix high-stake and low-stake providers in one bucket: the low-stake provider can copy from the other and risk little. Pick providers in different regions and track read latency over time. A virtual provider needs no special treatment here: compare it on stake like any other.

  2. Get a signed quote

    Call POST /negotiate on the provider (for a virtual provider, on one of its members) with owner, bucket (null for a new bucket, or the bucket id), max_bytes, duration, nonce (your current AgreementNonces value, read from chain at the best block), and replica_params for a replica. The response contains provider, the signed terms (AgreementTerms) and provider_signatures.

    The quote contains no price and no version. The chain reads price, replica sync price and stake from the provider at redemption and rejects the call with ProviderVersionMismatch if the provider's version differs from your expected_version. The version changes only when terms get worse (price or replica sync price up, stake down, a slash, or for a virtual provider a member leaving or k rising), so you never get an agreement on worse terms than you read.

    The quote is for one owner and one bucket target. You can redeem it once, until valid_until. Only one quote per nonce can be redeemed: on NonceMismatch, read the nonce again and request a new quote. At redemption the chain requires valid_until to be at most RequestTimeout (6 hours) after the current anchor block.

  3. Create the bucket

    One transaction: create_bucket_with_primary(provider, terms, sigs, expected_version, visibility) with a quote for bucket: null. Or two: create_bucket(min_providers, visibility), then add_primary_provider(bucket_id, provider, terms, sigs, expected_version) as admin with a quote for that bucket id.

    The pallet holds the provider's current price_per_byte × max_bytes × duration on the owner and records the provider's current stake in the agreement. The agreement gets an agreement_id (in StorageAgreementEstablished, or via agreement_info); you pass it to /commit, /commitment, /checkpoint-signature and challenge_offchain. Add more primaries the same way. Add members with set_member(bucket_id, member, role). If your wrapper lets the user skip the visibility choice, default to Private.

    Diagram: 06 Bucket creation and provider assignment

  4. Upload

    Split data into chunks of at most 256 KiB and build the Merkle tree. Call POST /exists with hashes, top-down, to skip subtrees the provider already has. Upload missing nodes bottom-up with PUT /node: a parent is rejected (children_missing) until its children exist. Uploads above the agreement quota fail with 507 quota_exceeded.

    Primaries do not sync with each other. Upload to every primary that must store the data. For a virtual provider, upload to one member; it distributes the data to the other members.

  5. Commit

    Call POST /commit with the agreement_id and the data_roots to append. The response contains the new mmr_root, start_seq, leaf_count, the leaf_indices of your roots, and provider_signatures (one entry from a physical provider, at least k from a virtual one). Store the signatures with the commitment and agreement_id: they are the evidence for challenge_offchain.

    Providers sign CommitmentPayload { version, genesis_hash, bucket_id, agreement_id, commitment }. The genesis hash is never transmitted: if you verify a signature yourself, insert the hash of the chain you connect to. A signature from another chain does not verify.

  6. Checkpoint

    Get signatures from each primary with GET /checkpoint-signature?bucket_id=…&agreement_id=…&prev_start_seq=…&prev_leaf_count=…, where prev_* is the on-chain snapshot. Then submit checkpoint(bucket_id, commitment, consistency_proof, signatures, activate_replacements) as a writer or admin. signatures is a list of (AccountId, Signature) pairs; at least min_providers signing slots must be present, and a virtual primary's members enter all-or-nothing (at least k or none). Set activate_replacements to false unless you activate a replacement (see Keep the data stored). Anyone can add more signatures to the current snapshot later with extend_checkpoint.

    The commitment must equal the snapshot's or append to it: same start_seq, larger leaf_count. For an append, pass the consistency_proof from one primary's response; it proves that the snapshot's MMR is a prefix of the new one. Otherwise pass none. A primary answers 400 prev_out_of_range if its state is behind the snapshot and 409 not_prefix if it holds a different branch. Every checkpoint needs min_providers on its own and replaces the snapshot's signers.

    Checkpoints are optional and can be infrequent. Without one, challenge_checkpoint is not available and providers must keep every state they signed.

    Diagram: 08 Upload, commit, checkpoint

  7. Read and verify

    Read with GET /read?data_root=…&offset=…&length=… or GET /node?hash=… from any primary or replica, and check each chunk against its hash. Every read is a storage check. In the background, fetch a few random chunks per week and record failures and latency per provider. Three random chunks per week detect a provider that deleted 10% of the data with 98% probability within 13 weeks.

    Diagram: 10 Read and verify

  8. Challenge a provider that does not serve

    Use a challenge only when the provider does not serve data. Pick the mode:

    • challenge_offchain(bucket_id, provider, agreement_id, commitment, target, provider_signatures): with the commit signatures. Pass commitment and agreement_id exactly as returned by /commit or /commitment; the chain rebuilds the signed payload from them. It fails with AgreementExpired once that agreement has expired, and with AgreementNotFound once its record is gone. Use it on buckets with frequent writes.
    • challenge_checkpoint: against the on-chain snapshot. Use it on buckets with infrequent writes. It fails if a new checkpoint no longer lists the provider.
    • challenge_replica: against the root a replica confirmed on-chain.

    challenge_offchain and challenge_replica fail with ChallengedLeafDeleted for a leaf below the snapshot's start_seq: an admin deleted it.

    You lock a deposit that covers the provider's response cost. The provider has ChallengeTimeout (48 hours) to respond; for a virtual provider, any member named in the agreement may respond. If nobody responds, anyone can call resolve_expired_challenge (free on success) to slash it. The chain rejects an invalid response and the challenge stays open. If the provider serves the data off-chain after all, call cancel_challenge; your deposit is refunded. The transaction pool drops a response that races your cancellation and charges no fee for it. Against a virtual provider, anyone can add further member signatures over the same commitment with extend_challenge, so every signer is slashed if the challenge times out. See Costs for who pays.

    Diagrams: 12 Challenge protocol, 13 Challenge, response and slash

  9. Keep the data stored

    • extend_agreement(bucket_id, provider, additional_duration, expected_version) before expiry, owner only. It pays the provider for the elapsed period and locks the payment for the added period at the provider's current price. The provider can block extensions (accepting_extensions, set_extensions_blocked). A provider whose stake is below MinProviderStake, for example after a slash, takes no new or extended agreement (StakeBelowMinimum).
    • top_up_agreement(bucket_id, provider, additional_bytes, expected_version) to raise the quota, owner only.
    • add_replica_provider(bucket_id, provider, terms, sigs, expected_version) to add a replica with a replica quote; anyone can fund its syncs with top_up_replica_sync_balance.
    • To change a primary without downtime, add the new primary first, upload the data to it, and checkpoint with its signature before the old agreement ends.
    • Virtual providers only: the owner can replace an agreement's member set without a gap with create_replacement(bucket_id, provider, additional_duration, expected_version) (additional_duration may be 0). The successor becomes live when it proves it has the data: for a primary, a checkpoint with activate_replacements set and signatures requested with the successor's agreement_id; for a replica, the replica's confirm_replica_sync with activate_replacement set. While a replacement is pending, extend_agreement and top_up_agreement fail with ReplacementPending, and it cannot be cancelled. If the old agreement expires first, the successor's payment returns to you (ReplacementExpired).

    Diagrams: 14 Changing a primary provider, 11 Replica sync

  10. Settle

    After expiry, the owner calls end_agreement with Pay or Burn within SettlementTimeout (48 hours). A burn costs the owner an extra premium (BurnPremium). After the window, the provider can call claim_expired_agreement and receive the payment. An open challenge does not block settlement: the challenge contains everything its resolution needs. No agreement ends early, except through a replacement on a virtual provider.

    A provider that cannot serve adequately may call refund_agreement at any time before settlement (AgreementRefunded): the remaining payment returns to the owner, the agreement continues until expiry, and the provider stays challengeable. At settlement there is nothing to pay or burn.

    When the last agreement ends, the bucket remains on-chain with its members and snapshot. The admin can add a new primary later and upload the data again.

    Diagram: 15 Agreement settlement

  11. Delete data or freeze

    An admin deletes leaves with POST /delete, the agreement_id and keep_from_seq. The provider starts a new MMR at the snapshot's end (start_seq + leaf_count), appends the snapshot's leaves from keep_from_seq to it again with new sequence numbers, and returns the new signed commitment. Every primary builds the same root; check it before you submit, because the chain cannot check the kept leaves. The deletion takes effect when an admin submits it as a checkpoint with no consistency proof; providers prune the deleted leaves only then.

    freeze_bucket makes the bucket append-only. Freezing cannot be undone. Admins can delete a frozen bucket only once it has no agreements left, primary or replica.

Private buckets

  • Private controls access only. Honest primaries serve reads only to members; the chain cannot enforce this. To keep data confidential, encrypt it on the client.
  • On-chain metadata is public on every bucket: MMR roots, sizes, leaf counts, checkpoints, challenge events. A challenge response posts the chunk on-chain.
  • On a private bucket, only members and primary-agreement owners can challenge primaries.
  • Replicas serve everyone and anyone can challenge them, on public and private buckets.
  • Changing a bucket from public to private does not recall data that replicas already have. Changing it from private to public discloses data from then on.

Provider HTTP API

JSON over HTTP. A local provider node listens on http://localhost:3333. A physical provider's address is in its on-chain endpoint; a virtual provider has none, so you call one of its members. Every endpoint that returns a provider signature returns provider_signatures, a list of { signer, signature }: one entry from a physical provider, at least k from a virtual one.

EndpointRole requiredUse
POST /negotiate—Get signed agreement terms
POST /exists—Find which hashes the provider is missing
PUT /nodeWriterUpload a chunk or internal node
POST /commitWriterAppend data_roots to the MMR under an agreement_id; returns a signed commitment
GET /checkpoint-signature—Signatures for the checkpoint call, by bucket_id and agreement_id; with prev_start_seq and prev_leaf_count, also the consistency_proof for an append
GET /commitment—Signed commitment for challenge_offchain, by bucket_id and agreement_id
GET /read, GET /nodeReader on a private bucketRead chunks
GET /mmr_proof, GET /chunk_proof—Proofs that a chunk is in a committed root
POST /deleteAdminStart a new MMR that keeps the snapshot's leaves from keep_from_seq, under an agreement_id
GET /health, GET /info—Status

The design requires a signed header for PUT /node, POST /commit, POST /delete and reads of access-controlled data. A dash means the design does not list a role.

Authentication

Authorization: Web3Storage <pubkey_hex>:<signature_hex>:<unix_timestamp>
Signed message: "web3storage:<METHOD>:<bucket_id>:<unix_timestamp>"

The signature is sr25519. The timestamp must be within 5 minutes of the provider's clock. The provider reads your role from bucket.members on-chain and caches it. A chain event clears the cache for that bucket; otherwise it expires after 30 seconds (--auth-cache-ttl). If a refetch fails, the provider uses the cached roles for up to 5 minutes (--auth-max-stale), then rejects requests with 503 membership_unavailable.

The design marks this scheme as under-specified: the signed message does not cover the request body or the provider. Tracked in #304.

SDKs

The SDKs wrap the calls above. They are not part of the design documents; their READMEs describe them.

  • TypeScript: @web3-storage/sdk, built on polkadot-api. Transaction semantics and read options are in its README.
  • Rust: storage client for the flow on this page.
  • Rust: file-system client and S3 client, which add drives, directories and objects on top of buckets.

For a short TypeScript example with the Web3Storage facade, see "Entry points" in the SDK README.
